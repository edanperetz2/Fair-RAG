pl_alpha_sweep_n30 (2026-07-24 to 2026-07-25)

IDEA / PARAMETERS: Follow-up to scoped_rq_experiment, aimed at fixing two underpowering problems diagnosed in that first run: only 2 of the paper's 4 Plackett-Luce alpha values (2, 8) had been sampled, and pl_samples=10 was far below the paper's own N=100 stochastic-sample size, both of which could distort EE-D bin assignments and bias the EE-D coefficient toward zero in the mediation test. The plan was to re-run BM25/flanT5Small/all-7-LaMP-tasks with the full alpha in {1,2,4,8} at N=30 (a deliberate compromise between the cheap N=10 and the paper's N=100, chosen for a ~9-hour budget). Execution deviated from this plan partway through: the laptop's active power scheme ("Legion Balance Mode") was capping GPU clocks to ~13% of max, making the run 2.5-4x slower than estimated even after switching to "Legion Performance Mode." Given a revised ~15-22 hour remaining estimate, the decision was made to stop the N=30 sweep after LaMP-1/2/3 (fully done) and LaMP-4 (3 of 4 alphas done), and finish LaMP-4's missing alpha=8 plus all of LaMP-5/6/7 at the cheaper N=10 instead - reusing whatever N=10 data already existed from the original scoped_rq_experiment run (which turned out to cover alpha=2 and alpha=8 for every task, so only alpha=1 and alpha=4 needed fresh computation for LaMP-5/6/7). Final composition: 49 run directories total, LaMP-1/2/3 at a clean N=30 four-point sweep, LaMP-4 with three alphas at N=30 and one at N=10, LaMP-5/6/7 at N=10 across all four alphas. Two real robustness bugs were found and fixed along the way: a Windows PermissionError during manifest checkpointing (OneDrive transiently locking a just-written file) that crashed the entire batch, fixed with retry-with-backoff; and BatchExperimentRunner previously aborting the whole multi-hour batch on any single setting's failure, now isolated so one bad setting doesn't cost the rest.

RESULTS / CONCLUSIONS: Analysis re-run on the deduplicated, best-available-precision dataset (42 settings, 3,906 query rows, up from 28/2,604 in the original run) via a new select_best_precision() step in experiments/analyze_scoped_results.py. The paper-style Table 2 replication is meaningfully better powered (most bins now have 20-200+ comparison queries versus as few as 2-7 before), and the qualitative pattern holds: utility deltas trend flat-or-positive as EE-D rises through [0.4,1.0), worst in [0.0,0.2). The ILD-EE-D correlation is now statistically significant with the larger sample (r=-0.045, p=0.005) but the effect size remains practically negligible - more data made a tiny real effect detectable, not bigger. The mediation test (n=3,552) shows EE-D's coefficient is not just weak but sign-unstable: +0.0034 (p=0.84) alone, -0.0077 (p=0.65) with ILD added - neither remotely significant, so the original run's "62% shrinkage" framing doesn't meaningfully replicate (that metric assumes monotonic shrinkage toward zero, which breaks down for a coefficient that's indistinguishable from zero throughout). ILD's effect (coef=-0.308, p=6.9e-08) holds up and gets more significant with more data. The most notable new finding is a borderline-significant EE-D x ILD interaction (coef=-0.316, p=0.050) suggesting EE-D's relationship with utility may be conditional on diversity level (positive at low ILD, weakening or reversing as ILD increases) rather than a fixed effect - not visible in the smaller original sample, and worth a dedicated, purpose-built follow-up before treating as more than a lead. Process lesson: check the active Windows power scheme (not just GPU idle/memory) before estimating multi-hour GPU workload timing on a laptop with OEM performance profiles.
